# One practice with a phone tutorial, measured

The opener of the video and the "13 minutes" card come from one real practice, recorded on 8 November 2026. This is how it was measured and what the numbers mean.

## Setup

- Jonathan practised the salsa right turn at home the way he normally does: a phone on the desk playing a YouTube tutorial from the school he learned at, "Learn Simple Salsa Turns (Beginner)" by Baila Productions Salsa Dance School (4 minutes 28 seconds long), with him facing the phone.
- The phone's screen was recorded over USB for the whole practice (scrcpy, 1080 by 2340, no audio so the phone's speaker stayed on), so every tap, seek and restart is in the file: `broll/measure-screen.mp4`, 13 minutes 17 seconds.
- A camera on a tripod, off to one side, filmed the room at the same time: `broll/dslr/MVI_0693.MOV` and `MVI_0694.MOV` (the camera split the file at 4 GB). The camera runs about 3.5 seconds ahead of the phone recording; the offset was read off the moment he pressed play and both restarts.
- He was told to practise without changing anything for the camera, and to rewind whenever he would normally rewind.

## Method

Every hard cut in the screen recording was listed by frame differencing (37 cuts) and each one was checked by eye against the frames before and after it. The tutorial's own cuts (its title cards, its "Let's Practice (With Music)" card, the end screen) were separated from the dancer's actions (taps on the player, seeks, restarts). The camera footage was checked at the same timestamps to confirm each walk to the phone.

An automatic rewind detector was tried first and dropped: the tutorial is one static camera on one instructor, so its frames match each other everywhere and the detector reported matches that were not seeks. The counts below are from the frame-by-frame check, not from the detector.

No seeks inside the video: he never scrubbed back to a section, he let the whole tutorial run and restarted it from the beginning each time to get another round of music.

## The numbers on the card

- **13 minutes with the tutorial**: 12 minutes 50 seconds from play (0:19) to the final end screen (13:09), rounded.
- **3 times the same 4:28 video**: three full plays.
- **2 walks to the phone to restart it**: at 4:30 and 8:47, each confirmed in the camera footage.
- **4 minutes of music to dance to**: three sections of 86 seconds, 258 seconds, 4.3 minutes. The other 8.5 minutes were the instructor talking and demonstrating, watched three times over.

## What it does and does not show

It shows how one dancer's home practice with a phone tutorial actually goes: most of the time is spent watching, the music runs for a third of it, and getting another round of music means walking to the phone. It is one practice by one person on one tutorial, not a study, and the video says so by calling it one practice, measured.

The tutorial's own structure is a big part of the result: a video with a longer music section would give a different split. The walks are the part that does not change with the video: the phone sits where it sits, and when it needs a tap, you go to it.
